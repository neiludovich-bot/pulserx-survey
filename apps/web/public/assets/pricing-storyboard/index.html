Pricing Storyboard

From ad hoc pricing to confident package design

This concept proposes a clearer mid-market pricing story: simpler tiers, stronger value anchors, and fewer approval bottlenecks when commercial teams need to move quickly.

Current friction

Inconsistent discounting and unclear packaging language slow decisions.

Core idea

Use a simpler tier ladder with a visible “best fit” option for the typical buyer.

Expected outcome

Better pricing confidence for sellers and cleaner conversations with finance.

Buyer Problem

Buyers want a faster answer to “what do I get at each level?”

Confusion

Feature bundles feel negotiable, so list price loses credibility.

Delay

Approvals stack up because exceptions are the norm instead of the edge case.

Leakage

Teams discount early when value signals are not obvious enough to defend price.

Concept

Three tiers with one visible recommendation

Foundation

$24k

  • Core workflow coverage
  • Standard reporting
  • Email support

Growth

$42k

  • Advanced reporting and automation
  • Priority onboarding support
  • Most common fit for 200-500 employee teams

Scale

$68k

  • Enterprise controls and governance
  • Multi-team administration
  • Dedicated strategic support

Message

The value story centers on predictability, not just lower price

The proposal frames price as a way to buy faster commercial clarity: fewer one-off decisions, stronger alignment between finance and go-to-market, and cleaner packaging explanations for customers.

“Make it easy for the commercial team to know when the standard offer is enough and when finance really needs to step in.”

Research Questions

Signals to listen for in the interview

  • Does the respondent believe the tier ladder would reduce pricing friction?
  • Which tier feels most credible for their organization today?
  • What objections would finance or leadership raise first?
  • Would the recommended tier framing feel helpful or manipulative?
